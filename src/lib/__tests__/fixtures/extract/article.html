Council approves transit plan

By A Reporter

The city council voted on Tuesday to approve a new transit plan that will add three bus lines and extend evening service across the northern districts, officials said after a five-hour meeting.

Supporters argued the plan would cut commute times for thousands of shift workers who currently rely on a single overnight route. Opponents questioned whether the budget could absorb the added cost without raising fares next year.

The transit authority estimates the expansion will cost about 40 million over four years, with half of the money coming from a regional grant that was announced last spring.

Council members amended the proposal to require a public review after the first twelve months, a change that won over two members who had planned to vote against it.

Construction of new shelters is expected to begin in January, and the first of the three lines could open as early as March if the grant money arrives on schedule.

The mayor called the vote a turning point for neighbourhoods that have waited years for reliable service. Residents who spoke at the meeting were divided, with several asking for more stops near the hospital.

Transit officials said they would publish detailed route maps next month and hold three open sessions where residents can suggest changes before the routes are finalised.

Fare levels will stay unchanged until at least the end of next year, the authority's chief executive told reporters, adding that a review of the fare structure would follow the public review.
